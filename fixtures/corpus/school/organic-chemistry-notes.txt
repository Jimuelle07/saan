ORGANIC CHEMISTRY II - Lecture 7 notes: nucleophilic substitution

SN2 (bimolecular)
  - One step, concerted. Nucleophile attacks from the back side as the leaving group leaves.
  - Rate = k[substrate][nucleophile]
  - Inversion of configuration (Walden inversion) at the stereocenter.
  - Favored by: methyl > primary > secondary. Tertiary basically never (steric hindrance).
  - Strong nucleophiles (I-, CN-, RS-, OH-), polar APROTIC solvents (DMSO, DMF, acetone).

SN1 (unimolecular)
  - Two steps: leaving group departs -> carbocation, then nucleophile attacks.
  - Rate = k[substrate] only.
  - Racemization (attack from either face of the planar carbocation), often slight excess inversion.
  - Favored by: tertiary > secondary. Carbocation stability, resonance (allylic, benzylic).
  - Weak nucleophiles (H2O, ROH), polar PROTIC solvents.
  - Watch for carbocation rearrangements: hydride and methyl shifts.

Leaving groups: good ones are weak bases. I- > Br- > Cl- >> F-. Tosylate is excellent.
OH- is bad -> protonate it or convert to tosylate first.

Competition with elimination (E1/E2)
  - Strong, bulky base (t-BuO-) -> E2
  - Heat favors elimination over substitution.

Exam tip: identify substrate degree first, then nucleophile strength, then solvent.
