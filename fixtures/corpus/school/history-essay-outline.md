# Essay outline: causes of the First World War

**Thesis:** The war was not caused by the assassination in Sarajevo alone, but by the interaction
of militarism, rigid alliance systems, imperial rivalry and nationalism, which turned a regional
crisis into a continental war within six weeks.

## I. Introduction
- Hook: June 28, 1914 - Archduke Franz Ferdinand shot by Gavrilo Princip.
- Context: a century of relative great-power peace since 1815.
- Thesis statement.

## II. Militarism
- Anglo-German naval race (Dreadnought, 1906).
- Mobilization plans tied to railway timetables; Schlieffen Plan required attacking France first.

## III. Alliance systems
- Triple Alliance vs Triple Entente.
- Blank cheque from Germany to Austria-Hungary (July 5-6).

## IV. Imperialism
- Moroccan crises of 1905 and 1911.
- Competition for colonies heightened distrust between Britain and Germany.

## V. Nationalism
- Pan-Slavism and Serbian nationalism in the Balkans.
- Austro-Hungarian fear of disintegration.

## VI. Counter-argument
- Fischer thesis: Germany deliberately sought war. Clark's "Sleepwalkers": shared responsibility.

## VII. Conclusion
- Restate thesis; note how the July Crisis shows the danger of inflexible commitments.

Sources: Clark (2012), MacMillan "The War That Ended Peace", Tuchman "The Guns of August".
