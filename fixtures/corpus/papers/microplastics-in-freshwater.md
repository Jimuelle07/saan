# Literature notes: microplastics in freshwater systems

Course: Environmental Science seminar. Presentation on the 14th.

## Definitions
- Microplastics: plastic particles smaller than 5 mm. Primary (manufactured small, e.g. microbeads)
  vs secondary (fragments from larger items, tire wear, synthetic textile fibers).

## Key findings from the readings
1. Concentrations in rivers vary over orders of magnitude, from under 1 to thousands of particles
   per cubic meter, highest downstream of wastewater treatment plants and urban areas.
2. Fibers dominate most samples - laundry of synthetic clothing is a major source.
3. Treatment plants remove 90%+ of particles from water, but they end up in sewage sludge,
   which is spread on farmland and can wash back into rivers.
4. Lake sediments act as long-term sinks; sediment cores show increases since the 1950s.
5. Freshwater fish and invertebrates ingest particles; effects include reduced feeding and growth,
   but evidence at environmentally realistic concentrations is mixed.

## Methods problems
- No standard sampling mesh size, so studies are hard to compare.
- Contamination from lab coats and air during processing; blanks are essential.
- Spectroscopy (FTIR, Raman) needed to confirm polymer type; visual counting overestimates.

## Questions for discussion
- Should washing machine filters be mandatory (as France has legislated)?
- How do microplastics carry other pollutants or pathogens?
